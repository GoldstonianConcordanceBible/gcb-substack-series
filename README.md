# The Testimony After the Sermon

This repository contains the official Substack series:

The Testimony After the Sermon

This is the living layer of the Goldstonian Concordance Bible (GCB).

Each entry is written after preaching and documents:

- What was preached
- What was lived in the moment
- What was missed or needs correction
- What was prayed after the message
- What God is continuing to form in the life of the messenger

This is not a polished archive.
This is formation in real time.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of this repository is to capture the transformation of the preacher through the act of preaching.

The sermon is not the end of the message.
The life after the sermon is the continuation of the message.

This series exists to document that continuation.

---

## Relationship to the GCB Ecosystem

This repository operates alongside:

- The doctrinal layer (Medium / structured theological outputs)
- The canonical index (GitHub / machine-readable scripture alignment)
- The video corpus (YouTube / Sermon on the Mount at The Rock)

Each layer serves a different function:

- Doctrine explains the Word
- Sermons proclaim the Word
- Testimony lives the Word

Together, they form a complete system of:

Mirror → Water → Fire

---

## Framework

All entries align to the formation sequence:

Surrender → Abide in Christ → Mirror → Water → Fire

Each article reflects a position within that progression.

---

## Series Structure

Each sermon generates one Substack article.

Each article follows a fixed reflection structure:

1. What I Was Trying to Say
2. What I Actually Lived While Preaching It
3. What I Got Right
4. What I Should Have Corrected
5. What I Prayed About Afterward
6. What God Is Teaching Me Now
7. The Fifth Gospel Reflection

---

## Core Principle

You do not become the Fifth Gospel by preaching once.
You become it by living what you preach daily.

---

## Positioning

Medium carries structured doctrine through Professor Enoch.

This repository carries personal testimony in the voice of the preacher.

This distinction is intentional:

- Doctrine must remain stable
- Testimony must remain honest
